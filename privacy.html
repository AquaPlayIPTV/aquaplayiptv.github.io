Privacy Policy

AquaPlay IPTV · last updated 25 November 2026

In short: AquaPlay processes your M3U addresses, playlists and viewing habits locally, on your own device. We do not log, track, or upload your playlist contents or viewing history to our servers. Data leaves your device only in the cases described below, each one something you choose: your IPTV provider, Google Play for Premium, the encrypted relay when you use your phone, and Simkl, OpenSubtitles or TheIntroDB when you turn them on.

AquaPlay has no accounts, no sign-up, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. It contains no analytics or advertising SDKs; the Android app uses Google Play's billing library, to sell Premium.

What you put into the app stays on your device. The exceptions are the ones you choose: buying Premium through Google Play on Android TV, or on our licence site for the AquaPlay.apk from our website, and using your phone to send things to the TV. Each is described below. On a Samsung TV, AquaPlay is free and has nothing to buy.

What the app stores, and where

AquaPlay keeps your data on the device itself, in the app's own private storage. That includes:

  • the playlist details you enter — server address, username and password for an Xtream Codes account, or an M3U URL
  • your favourites, recently watched channels, channel numbers you have reassigned, and groups you have hidden
  • profiles, if you add them: each one's name, favourites, history and locks
  • your preferences: language, theme, text size, picture size, buffer settings, reminders, the parental PIN and channel locks
  • where you stopped in a film or episode, so you can continue from there; which films and episodes you have watched, and which episode each series is up to
  • the subtitle and soundtrack you chose for a channel, film or series, how far you moved its subtitles, and an intro you marked for a series
  • if you add a playlist from a file, the playlist's contents
  • for a large playlist of films and series, a copy of its list (names, posters, stories and episodes) in the app's own storage, so it opens quickly; it is replaced when the list is read again and removed with the playlist
  • recordings, if you make them: on Android TV in the app's own storage on the device, and on a PC in the folder you choose
  • on Android TV, your Premium status: when a free trial started, and whether Premium was bought
  • on a Samsung TV, the number of days the app has been opened, and when it last asked you about a donation — so it asks no more than once every two weeks
  • on a Samsung TV, the newest version of AquaPlay it has heard of, and which one it last told you about
  • a crash report: what the app was doing in the minutes before it last closed unexpectedly — the screen, the kind of stream that was playing, memory use, errors, the settings that affect playback, the TV's model and software version, how much memory it had free, how the last five sessions ended and, on a Samsung TV, the names of the other apps open at the time (a set short of memory closes apps). It never holds your playlist address, your account, or channel and programme names

None of this is sent to us. There is no account and no remote backup of your data, so we cannot see it, and cannot recover it for you if it is lost.

Who your details are sent to

Your playlist credentials are sent to one place only: the IPTV provider whose address you entered. That is what makes the app work — it asks your provider for your channel list, guide and streams, exactly as you asked it to.

AquaPlay ships with no channels and no playlists, and is not affiliated with any provider. Your relationship with your provider, and their handling of your data, is between you and them — their own privacy policy applies to it, not this one.

If your playlist supplies channel logos or a programme guide hosted elsewhere, your device fetches those directly from whichever servers your provider points at. Those servers will see your IP address, as with any request your device makes.

Using your phone with the TV

On a television you can use your phone to add a playlist, to send your playlists and settings to another TV, to save a backup file, and to restore one. Scanning the code on the TV opens a page at aquaplayiptv.github.io (dolev24.github.io for versions before 1.0.30), which GitHub serves and so receives your phone's IP address. The code carries a one-time key that encrypts what is sent before it travels through ntfy.sh, a public relay, which is how it works whatever network your phone is on. The key is never sent anywhere, so neither GitHub nor the relay can read what you sent; the relay only ever holds the encrypted message. When a playlist is sent this way, the TV answers the phone through the same relay, locked with the same key: whether it connected, and if not, what went wrong. That answer never contains your username or password. A playlist file chosen on the phone travels the same way, encrypted on the phone before it leaves; the relay keeps that encrypted file for up to three hours, then deletes it. This is the same on Samsung and Android TV.

A backup file holds your playlist details, passwords included, and is saved wherever you choose on your phone or PC. Keep it private. A backup, and sending to another TV, also carry what you have watched and where you stopped. A playlist added from a file stays on the TV it was added to.

The crash report in Settings → Advanced → Crash report has a code too. It opens aquaplayiptv.github.io/report.html with the report in the part of the address after the #, which your phone's browser keeps to itself and does not send to GitHub. The page only shows the report, so you can copy it, share it or email it to us if you choose. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

Settings → Share my settings works the same way: its code opens aquaplayiptv.github.io/h with, after the #, the AquaPlay version, the kind of TV, the language, the kind of playlist and how many channels it has, how much of the guide arrived, the Premium status, the settings you changed from how they come, the name of the channel playing and what its stream carries, recent pauses and errors (with any address taken out), the time zone, and how many connections the account allows. Never an address, username, password, PIN or playlist name. You decide whether to send it on.

Premium, and donations

On Android TV, Premium is bought through Google Play, and Google handles the payment under its own privacy policy. The app asks Google Play which AquaPlay purchases your Google account holds, so Premium can be turned on. We do not receive your name, your email address or any payment details. Google Play promo codes are redeemed in Google Play.

AquaPlay also has promo codes of its own, given away and never sold. Each is made for one TV's Device ID, the eight characters shown under Premium → Promo code. On Android TV the Device ID is made on the box from its Android ID, scrambled so that the Android ID itself never leaves the box. The code is checked on the TV itself, with nothing sent anywhere; Premium from it is kept on that TV only. If you ask us for a code you send us that Device ID, and nothing else is needed. A code can also be sent from your phone, through the same encrypted relay as a playlist (above).

On a Samsung TV, AquaPlay is free, with nothing to buy. Settings has a Support AquaPlay page showing a code that opens AquaPlay's page on Ko-fi on your phone, where you can leave a donation if you want to. The TV itself never contacts Ko-fi. A donation is handled by Ko-fi and its payment providers under their own privacy policies, and we see only what Ko-fi shows any creator about a supporter.

Premium bought on our website (the AquaPlay.apk)

If you install AquaPlay from our website instead of Google Play, you buy Premium on our licence site, aquaplay.aquaplay-licence.workers.dev. This is the one case where AquaPlay talks to a server of ours, and only once you open Premium and choose Buy Premium.

  • What the TV sends: a Device ID, a proof made from its Device key (never the key itself), that it is an Android device, and the AquaPlay version. The Device ID is made on the box from its Android ID, scrambled so that the Android ID never leaves the box. After you pay, the TV asks the site for its licence about every six hours.
  • What we keep: for each device, the Device ID, a hash of its proof, its plan and a short history (first contact, purchase, refund); for each payment, PayPal's order number, the plan, the amount and currency, the date and the email address PayPal gives us for the payer.
  • What we never receive: your card or bank details, which stay with PayPal, and your playlists or what you watch.
  • Who processes it: PayPal takes the payment under its own privacy statement; Cloudflare hosts the licence site and sees the IP address of anyone who opens it.
  • How long: payment records for as long as the law requires us to keep accounts; a device's record while its licence is in use, and deleted on request after that.

On a PC there is nothing to buy.

A Samsung TV also reads a small file on AquaPlay's website (aquaplayiptv.github.io/premium.json) a minute after starting and every six hours after, which says whether AquaPlay on Samsung is still free. The request carries nothing about you; GitHub sees the TV's IP address, as any website would.

Settings has a Join the Discord code too: it opens AquaPlay's Discord on your phone. The TV itself never contacts Discord.

Checking for a newer version

On a Samsung TV, once a day, AquaPlay asks GitHub which version of it is the newest (api.github.com, about the public AquaPlayIPTV/aquaplay-samsung repository), so it can tell you when an update is out. From version 1.0.54 the TV also downloads that version from the same public repository (github.com) and installs it in its own storage, so it updates itself. The requests carry nothing about you, your playlists or what you watch; GitHub sees your TV's IP address, as any website would, under its own privacy policy. You can choose in Settings → Advanced → New versions: update automatically, only say so, or off (on versions before 1.0.54, Check for new versions). On Android TV, Google Play takes care of updates, and the app asks nothing.

Skipping intros

When you watch an episode of a series, AquaPlay can offer a button to skip its intro or recap, or to go on to the next episode in the credits. The times come from TheIntroDB (api.theintrodb.org), a free, community-made database. To ask, the app sends it the series' TMDB number (a public film and TV database's number, from your provider's series information), the season and episode numbers, and the length of the file — nothing about you, your playlist or your provider. TheIntroDB sees your device's IP address, as any website would, under its own privacy policy. Nothing is asked for live channels or films. It is experimental and off unless you turn it on in Settings → Playback → Skip intros.

A series from an M3U playlist comes without its TMDB number, so with Skip intros on AquaPlay looks it up by the series' name on Wikidata (www.wikidata.org), once for each series, and keeps it on the device. For an anime's first season that TheIntroDB has nothing for, it asks AniSkip (api.aniskip.com) instead, with the anime's MyAnimeList number, the episode number and the episode's length. Only the series' name or numbers are sent, never anything about you; both see your device's IP address, as any website would.

Subtitles found online

Only when you choose Find subtitles online in a film's or an episode's Audio and subtitles menu, AquaPlay asks OpenSubtitles (api.opensubtitles.com) for subtitles: it sends the film's or series' title, its year or season and episode numbers (or its TMDB number, where your provider gives one) and the languages to look in. When you pick one, the app downloads that file. Nothing about you, your playlist or your provider is sent, and no account is used. OpenSubtitles sees your device's IP address, as any website would, and counts the downloads made from it each day, under its own privacy policy.

Simkl

If you sign in to Simkl (api.simkl.com) in Settings → Playback → Simkl, AquaPlay adds each film or episode you watch past nine tenths of it to that Simkl account's history: its title, its year or season and episode numbers, and its TMDB number where your provider gives one. You sign in on simkl.com with a code shown on the screen, so AquaPlay never sees your Simkl password; the app keeps the sign-in on this device only (not in backups, not in Share my settings). Nothing is sent before you sign in, and live channels are never sent. Signing out in the same place stops it. Simkl keeps what it is sent under its own privacy policy.

AquaPlay contacts no servers other than the ones described on this page.

Permissions

The Android TV app requests these permissions:

  • Internet — to reach your provider, and Google Play
  • Network state — to notice when the connection drops, so a stream can be reconnected
  • Keep awake — so the television does not sleep while you are watching or recording
  • Foreground service and notifications — so a recording carries on when you leave the app, with a notification while it runs
  • Exact alarms and start at boot — so a scheduled recording starts on time, even after the TV has been restarted
  • Google Play billing — to buy Premium (the copy from our website buys on our licence site instead, over the Internet permission)

On a Samsung TV, the app uses only the permissions to use the network, play video, read the remote, and save a file in its own storage. That file is a channel's list of qualities, for a channel that needs special headers, and it is deleted as soon as it has been read.

When you add a playlist from a file, AquaPlay reads that file and nothing else: on a Samsung TV it looks for playlist files on a USB stick plugged into the TV and reads the one you choose; on Android TV the system's own file chooser hands over only the file you pick.

AquaPlay does not request access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone or accounts, reads no file you did not choose, and does not use the advertising identifier.

Children

AquaPlay is a general-purpose media player and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age. The channels available in it are determined entirely by the playlist you supply; the app includes a parental PIN and channel locks.

Removing your data

You remove everything stored on the device by clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app; on Android TV that includes recordings in the app's storage. On Android TV that is Settings → Apps → AquaPlay → Clear data. Within the app, removing a playlist deletes its stored credentials immediately. Recordings made on a PC stay in the folder you chose until you delete them.

Premium bought through Google Play belongs to your Google account; you manage or cancel a subscription in Google Play, under Payments & subscriptions.

Premium bought on our website belongs to its Device ID: uninstalling AquaPlay does not remove it, and on the same device it comes back when you open Premium and choose Buy Premium, with nothing to pay. Write to us to have the licence site's record for your device deleted; payment records we must keep by law stay.

This website

aquaplay.live is a static website served by GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts, and serves its own fonts, so reading it contacts no third party. GitHub, which hosts the pages, receives your IP address as any web host does, under GitHub's own privacy statement. Because nothing on the site needs your consent, it shows no cookie banner. If that ever changes, we will ask for consent before anything optional loads.

Your rights

Under the GDPR, the CCPA and Israel's Privacy Protection Law you may ask what personal data we hold about you, and have it corrected or deleted. We hold none on servers of our own, except what Premium bought on our website describes for people who buy there: your data is on your device, and Removing your data above says how to delete it. If you email us, or send us a Device ID for a promo code, we keep that message in our inbox; ask and we delete it. We do not sell or share personal information.
